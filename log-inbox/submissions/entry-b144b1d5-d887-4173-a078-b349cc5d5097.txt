Evidence and billing conclusions
Oracle's public price list explicitly states paid tenancies receive 3,000 A1 OCPU-hours and 18,000 GB-hours monthly, shared across A1 bare metal, VMs, and container instances. Its Always Free documentation instead states 1,500 OCPU-hours and 9,000 GB-hours for all tenancies. These primary sources remain inconsistent; paid-tenancy wording supports 4 OCPU continuous operation, but actual account entitlement needs verification. Sources: https://www.oracle.com/cloud/price-list/ and https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm

Arithmetic: one VM held at 4 allocated OCPUs consumes at most 4 * 24 * 31 = 2,976 OCPU-hours during a 31-day calendar month. Corresponding totals are 2,880 for 30 days, 2,784 for 29 days, and 2,688 for 28 days. CPU saturation from Android or ALAS cannot increase allocated OCPU-hours. Flex permits configuration changes; workload demand does not by itself resize the instance. Additional A1 resources or resizing can increase tenancy-wide consumption. Previous response overemphasized the 24 OCPU-hour margin as a risk for a fixed solitary VM; under those fixed assumptions the CPU quantity fits 3,000 hours by construction. Sources: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Compute/References/computeshapes.htm and price list above.

31-day memory totals: 4 OCPU / 16 GB uses 11,904 GB-hours; 4 OCPU / 24 GB uses 17,856 GB-hours. A 2 OCPU / 12 GB fallback uses 1,488 OCPU-hours and 8,928 GB-hours, fitting both published allowances. These calculations assume no other shared usage and unchanged allocation. Recommended production candidate, subject to entitlement and runtime validation: one VM.Standard.A1.Flex at 4 OCPUs / 16 GB. The 24 GB alternative also fits the advertised paid allowance. CPU pressure, not demonstrated memory pressure, motivates the four-core recommendation. E2.1.Micro is unsuitable for this qualified ARM64 Android workload; other ARM shapes do not inherit A1 allowances by assumption.

Oracle FAQ confirms upgrading during the trial preserves remaining trial credit until original expiry or exhaustion, whichever comes first. A zero payable bill during trial does not establish ongoing free eligibility: inspect metered quantities, service charges, and promotional-credit consumption. September and October have separate monthly allowances; the interval through October 9 alone does not prove an entire 31-day cycle. Source: https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/

Proposed cost controls
Configure allocation limits in OCI, not just Docker CPU caps, FPS caps, or process affinity. Permit one A1 deployment location and constrain aggregate CPU/memory; A1 quotas are availability-domain scoped, so duplicating a four-core allowance across domains would allow more than four overall. Routine automation should operate the existing instance without resource creation, resizing, or quota-changing privileges. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Quotas/Concepts/resourcequotas_topic-Compute_Quotas.htm

Start with about 80-100 GB total boot/block storage only if a disk inventory confirms this fits working and rollback requirements. Current Always Free guide states 200 GB combined boot/block storage and five backups in the home region, including eligible paid accounts. Confirm actual volume eligibility and performance settings. Count retained volumes, clones, and backups; avoid automatic expansion and unbounded retention. Keep browser streaming on demand and rotate logs. Memory, storage, backups, traffic, and optional services require separate controls from OCPU allocation. Budgets are soft limits, with alerts evaluated every 24 hours, not a hard spending cap. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Billing/Concepts/budgetsoverview.htm
